# Map Reading

Use a topographic map to relate the features you can see to the route
you intend to take. Practise on a familiar marked track with a printed
map before relying on the technique away from tracks.

## Read the map margin

- Check the edition date and confirm that the sheet covers your route.
  A printed track or crossing can change after the map is made.
- Use the legend to distinguish tracks, roads and waterways. Read the
  contour interval and units before estimating elevation changes.
- Find the scale bar. At 1:25,000, 4 cm represents 1 km horizontally.
  If the map has been resized, use the scale bar printed with it
  instead of measuring against the original scale ratio.

## Read the terrain

A contour joins points at the same elevation. The contour interval is
the change in elevation between adjacent contour levels. When reading
one map with a fixed interval, contours packed closely together show a
steeper slope than contours spaced farther apart.

Use contours alongside the legend and visible features. For example,
a track that crosses several tightly spaced contours in a short
distance may be steeper than a longer route along the slope. The map
distance does not include extra walking from climbing or detours.

## Check your position

1. Pick a feature you can identify on both the ground and the map, such
   as a signed track junction.
2. Turn the map so nearby mapped features lie in the directions you see
   them. Confirm the orientation with another feature. For compass
   orientation, see [Compass Use](compass_use.md).
3. Pick the next junction or recognisable feature before moving. Check
   that the direction and terrain fit the map as you go. If they do
   not, pause and recheck your last confirmed position.
