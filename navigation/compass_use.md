# Compass Use

A baseplate compass helps you measure and follow a direction. Practise
first between two visible points in a familiar area. If your compass
has an adjustable declination setting, check its instructions before
using the exercise below.

## Take a magnetic bearing

For this exercise, use a compass with declination adjustment set to
zero. Hold it flat, away from vehicles, steel and magnets. Aim the
travel arrow at a visible landmark. Rotate the dial to align its north
mark with the north-seeking end of the needle, then read the degrees
at the index mark. That number is the bearing from magnetic north.

To practise following the same bearing, leave the dial where it is.
Turn yourself until the needle lines up with the dial's north mark
again. The travel arrow should point towards the landmark.

## Match the bearing to the map

- **True north** is the direction towards the geographic North Pole.
- **Grid north** follows the north direction of the map's grid lines.
- **Magnetic north** is the reference for an uncorrected compass
  bearing.

A bearing measured from the map grid and one measured from the compass
use different references. When transferring between them, use the
local grid-to-magnetic angle and the conversion direction shown on the
map or in the compass instructions. Check the date and area of the
angle before relying on it: magnetic direction varies by place and
changes over time. If you cannot establish the correction, keep
practice to visible landmarks.

For choosing a scale and checking terrain, see [Map Reading](map_reading.md).
